## a. Wind interconnection price factor

This implements option (ii) of `interconnection_headroom/docs/wind_price_check.md` in its exact form.

**How it works:**
- Wind keeps its 0.25 MW of room per MW.
- It pays f × the step price per MW of nameplate, through its own share of each step (`ICStepPriced`) and of the
  release (`ICReleasePriced`).
- The share adds (f / 0.25 − 1) × the step price on top of the step's own cost.
- `switch_case.py` writes `ic_price_factors.csv` only when a factor is set. With no factor, the case inputs are
  identical to v3.1.

**Toy test:** run at no factor, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.3. Steps fill in cost order, and wind pays f × the step price per
MW.
- Example: North 2030, f = 1.0. Wind fills the 20 $/kW step (0.4 MW of room) and then the 40 $/kW step (0.8 MW).
- The steps cost $40k on their own. Wind pays 4 × $40k = $160k, which is $33.3/kW across its 4.8 MW: 1.0 × the
  mean step price.

**Known limit:** within a period, the LP gives wind the cheapest steps it reaches. Where wind and other
technologies share a zone's steps in the same period, wind's charge is therefore at the low end.

## b. Battery storage credit with the OBBBA phase-down

**v3.1 (`flat`):** the `tax_credits` axis sets `atb_modifiers.batteries` capex ×0.7 (the 30% 48E ITC) in every
period. With the Section 232 tariff (`batteries_tariff`, ×1.20), new batteries cost 0.84 × ATB.

**`obbba`:**
- The axis's ×0.7 becomes ×1.0.
- The same 30% credit goes to the credit writer as an investment term (`tax_credit_terms.storage_itc_48e`,
  `kind: investment`).
- New-build battery capex (power and energy) in `gen_build_costs.csv` becomes × (1 − 0.30 × phase).
- The phase is 1 for build years through 2033, 0.75 for 2034, 0.5 for 2035 and 0 from 2036.
- An axis value with no storage credit (`no_wind_solar_storage`, `repealed`) stays without one.
- Existing batteries' build costs are untouched.

**The credit writer:** this is the §90 writer, `s0_workflow/tax_credits.py`. It was brought in from 6adc228 with
its `pg_to_switch` hook. It is inert for S0: the test compares its `gen_tax_credits.csv` with the s0-v3.1
writer's, byte for byte, under both storage settings.

**Phase basis:**
- `period_label`: the period's label year, as the production credits use.
- `span_mean`: the mean phase over the period's build years (`period_spans`). This matters only for the 2035
  stage, which covers builds in 2031–35.

**Battery capex by stage (multiple of ATB Moderate):**

| Stage (build years) | flat | obbba, period_label | obbba, span_mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2028 (2026–28) | 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.70 |
| 2030 (2029–30) | 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.70 |
| 2035 (2031–35) | 0.70 | 0.85 | 0.745 |
| 2040 (2036–40) | 0.70 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 2045 (2041–45) | 0.70 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| With the ×1.20 tariff | 0.84 every stage | 0.84 / 0.84 / 1.02 / 1.20 / 1.20 | 0.84 / 0.84 / 0.894 / 1.20 / 1.20 |

**$/kW:** the dollar values need a case build on the VM, because ATB costs come from the PowerGenome database.
The build writes `investment_credit_report.csv`, with mean $/MW before and after the credit by technology and
stage.

**Not modelled:**
- Construction-start timing: a project that starts by 2033 can enter service later at full credit, which favours
  `span_mean` or later.
- OBBBA's foreign-entity-of-concern (FEOC) sourcing limits.
- Geothermal's 48E. It has the same OBBBA phase-down but stays at ×0.7 in `resources.yml`.

## c. WA import charge: two-tranche

**v3.1 (`per_mwh`):** every MWh delivered into a WA zone (p1–p4) from outside CA and WA pays price × 0.437.

**`two_tranche`:**
- WA's border directions leave `trans_import_cost.csv` and form a named tranche "WA"
  (`trans_import_tranches.csv` and `trans_import_tranches_dirs.csv`).
- It uses the same LP form as CA: `wa_two_tranche.free_mwh_per_yr` is free, and every MWh above it pays
  price × 0.437.
- `study_modules.trans_hurdle_cost` takes any number of named tranches, all in `TxImportTrancheCost`. CA's
  single-tranche files are unchanged.
- With free = 0, the result equals v3.1's charge: same delivered MWh and same total cost on the toy.

**Proposed WA free tranche: the charged block should equal WA's unspecified purchases, 13.2 TWh/yr, so set**
`free_mwh_per_yr = WA's modelled 2028 imports − 13.2 TWh`, held flat.
- **Source:** Washington Department of Commerce, *Electric Utility Fuel Mix Disclosure Report, CY2023*
  (February 2025). The statewide mix gives unspecified at 14.85% of 89.14 TWh, which is 13.2 TWh. The rest:
  hydro 48.99%, natural gas 12.40%, wind 10.73%, coal 6.58%, nuclear 4.32%, solar 0.94%.
- **Check before use:** these figures come from the report as quoted in search results. The Commerce site and the
  PUD-hosted copy are blocked by this environment's network policy, so the 89.14 TWh total should be read from
  the report itself.
- **Why this basis:** Ecology's CCA electric-power-entity data (specified vs unspecified imported MWh) would be
  the direct source, but it isn't published in a form I could reach. In Commerce's fuel mix, "unspecified" is
  WA's power with no identified source. Under the CCA, that power is charged at the 0.437 default. Specified
  imports carry their own factors: federal (BPA) hydro is near zero, and Colstrip coal is high.
- **What's needed from the VM:** WA's delivered imports over the WA border directions in the S0 v3.1 2028 stage.
  With that number, the free tranche is fixed.
- **Caveats:**
  - Commerce's "unspecified" also covers in-state market purchases from other utilities, so 13.2 TWh is an upper
    bound on unspecified imports.
  - The model's WA zones and the Commerce retail footprint aren't identical.
- **Interim setting:** keep the flag off. Its free = 0 setting reproduces v3.1.

## d. CA free-tranche calibration (proposal; not implemented)

The S0 v3.1 tranche is 49.4 TWh/yr (49,399 GWh), flat. It comes from CEC's 2023 Total System Electric Generation
(TSEG) imports of 76,400 GWh, by fuel:

| Fuel | Import GWh |
|---|---|
| Large hydro | 5,821 |
| Nuclear | 8,558 |
| Wind + solar + small hydro | 35,020 |
| Geothermal | 2,569 |
| Biomass | 753 |
| Natural gas | 8,582 |
| Coal | 4,724 |
| Unspecified | 10,373 |

The tranche counts large hydro, nuclear, wind, solar and small hydro: 5,821 + 8,558 + 35,020 = 49,399.

**Source: CEC vs MRR.** The carbon charge is CARB's, and CARB's Mandatory Reporting Regulation (MRR) decides
what is specified.
- **MRR:** an import counts as specified only with a specified-source contract and direct delivery, usually shown
  on e-tags. MRR does not let an importer report specified power as unspecified.
- **CEC TSEG:** counts contracted out-of-state renewables as wind or solar imports. That includes firmed-and-shaped
  (PCC2) renewables, which physically arrive as unspecified power.
- **So:** MRR's unspecified volume is larger than CEC's.
- **Indicator:** CARB inventory figures quoted by Next10 for 2022 put unspecified imports plus T&D at 8.9 MMTCO2e
  against 8.6 MMTCO2e for specified imports. At 0.428 t/MWh, 8.9 MMT is at most 20.8 TWh unspecified, about
  twice CEC's 10.4 TWh.
- **Proposal:** use MRR's latest reporting year when the CARB data can be read. CARB's site was not reachable from
  here. The 20.8 TWh indicator is an upper bound: it includes transmission and distribution losses and is a
  different year.

**Geothermal and large hydro:** count both.
- **Large hydro:** specified imports from hydro carry a zero factor under MRR. Being outside the RPS doesn't matter
  for the carbon charge.
- **Geothermal:** specified geothermal carries its plant's own low factor, which is close to zero next to 0.428.
  Leaving its 2.6 TWh in the charged block overstates the charge.
- **Biomass:** its CO2 is biogenic, so count it as well (0.75 TWh).
- **Emitting specified imports:** specified gas and coal (13.3 TWh) stay in the charged block. The default 0.428
  is below coal's factor, so the block slightly understates their charge.

**Trajectory to 2045:**
- Hold the 2028 value flat to 2030.
- From 2035, scale it with clean supply in the exporting WECC regions, by one of two forms:
  1. **Exogenous index (recommended):** the ratio of non-CA WECC clean generation (hydro, nuclear, wind, solar,
     geothermal) in the stage year to 2023, from an outside projection (EIA AEO WECC regions) or from the S0 v3.1
     run's own exporting zones. It's simple and keeps the charge exogenous.
  2. **Endogenous:** free tranche = α × the model's clean generation in exporting zones, with
     α = 2023 free tranche ÷ 2023 clean generation. This is linear, so it fits the same LP. But it rewards building
     out-of-state clean power in the model, which is real-world behaviour for specified contracts but also a new
     incentive the model would act on. Keep it as a sensitivity.
- Palo Verde (8.6 TWh of the nuclear imports) is contracted well into the 2040s, so the nuclear part needn't
  decline before 2045.

**Candidates and what they imply for 2028 imports.** In the S0 v3.1 run the tranche bound in every stage, and
imports above it were zero (`s0_production.md`, "Import charge: v3.2 items", item 2). So CA's imports sat at the
kink: the model imports exactly the free volume, and the next MWh would pay the full rate ($20.80/MWh in 2028,
48.6 × 0.428).
- **At the kink, 2028 CA imports follow the free tranche about one-for-one,** and the CA import charge stays at
  about zero.
- **That holds while the kink stays where imports stop.** A tranche larger than the economic import level would
  stop binding. Check it with the runner's 2028 `trans_import_tranche_results.csv` after a rebuild.

| Candidate | Free 2028 (TWh/yr) | Basis | 2028 CA imports (at the kink) | vs v3.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 (v3.1) | 49.4 | CEC: hydro, nuclear, wind, solar | ≈ 49.4 TWh | — |
| D2 | 52.7 | CEC: D1 + geothermal 2.57 + biomass 0.75 | ≈ 52.7 TWh | +3.3 TWh, displacing in-state generation (mostly gas at the margin) |
| D3 | ≈ 39 (indicative) | MRR: D1 less the extra unspecified MRR counts (≈ 20.8 − 10.4 TWh) | ≈ 39 TWh | −10.4 TWh |

All three are below CEC's 2023 total imports of 76.4 TWh. The v3.1 run's zero unspecified imports, against about
27 TWh historically (item 2), points to the charge above the tranche, not to the tranche size.

**Recommendation:** D2 as v3.2's value, with the WECC-index trajectory. Replace it with D3's MRR-based number
once CARB's MRR importer data for the latest year is read.

## e. p1 (WA) solar profiles (proposal; not implemented)

**What the data show.** The full hourly profiles are on the VM. The committed fedpol case
(`switch/in/foresight/s4x1_fedpol_current`) holds 5 sample days from weather years 2008, 2011, 2012 and 2013.
`s0_workflow/scripts/check_vre_profiles.py` (now on this branch) gives:

| New-build PV vs the other WA zones' PV, same hours | 2008 | 2011 | 2012 | 2013 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p1 cluster 1 | 0.87 | 0.65 | 0.47 | 0.21 |
| p1 cluster 2 | 0.18 | 0.14 | 0.46 | 0.17 |

- **Neighbours' two clusters agree.** In p2, p3, p4, p6, p8 and p9, the two new-build clusters agree with each
  other within about 0.05 of daily mean CF on every day.
- **Existing p1 solar is normal:** 0.26–0.44 daily mean on the four non-winter days, close to p3's.
- **No time-zone shift:** first, last and peak hours match the neighbours within an hour.
- **No missing data:** no daylight hours where neighbours produce and p1 reads 0.

**Cause.** The fault is in p1's new-build cluster profiles, not in the weather years or the time handling:
- Only p1's two clusters diverge from each other and from the zone's existing solar.
- Their metadata capacity factor (0.23) is higher than their hourly profiles give.
- The divergence changes from year to year (cluster 1 runs 0.87 in 2008 and 0.21 in 2013).

So the hourly profiles attached to p1's new-build clusters don't belong to the cells whose metadata describes
them.

The most likely mechanism is that the reV profile rows for p1's cells are joined by position, not by site id
(gid), in the weather years whose files order the sites differently. That would give the pattern you see: two
years right, five wrong. A scaling or units error would give a constant ratio, which isn't what the data show.

I can't confirm the mechanism here. The PowerGenome resource files are on the VM, and the PowerGenome clone is
off limits.

**Decisive test on the VM:**
1. For p1's cluster cells, compute each cell's per-year mean CF from the hourly profile file, and compare it with
   its supply-curve `mean_cf`.
2. Matched cells give a ratio of about 1 in every year. Mismatched years show the 0.2–0.7 ratios.
3. Also compare the gid order of the 2007–2013 profile files.

**Fix:**
- Rebuild p1's new-build PV profiles with a gid-based join, or regenerate them from the per-year reV files.
- Run `check_vre_profiles.py` on the rebuilt case and confirm the ratio to peers is about 1 for every weather
  year.
- If S0 needs a stopgap before that, give p1's new-build clusters the hourly shape of p1's existing PV, scaled to
  each cluster's metadata CF. This is a placeholder and should be flagged as one.

**Weather years in S0:** all seven of PowerGenome's 2007–2013 365-day years (`time_sampling.first_weather_year:
2007`). The fleet-independent day selection draws its sample and tail days from the whole record. If the
runner's profiles are right in only two years, most S0 sample days carry the low p1 profiles. The day list and
weights are in each case's `time_sampling/<year>/` diagnostics on the VM.

**Size of the effect:** about 86 GW of p1 new-build PV potential produces roughly 20–80% of what it should on most
sample days. The model therefore builds little p1 solar and replaces it with other resources. The effect on S0
needs the VM run.

## Design notes: fidelity beyond option B

These are not implemented.
- **Regional split models** (`region_scope`): a detail region at zone level with the rest aggregated. These can afford
  more sample days, because the model is smaller, so zone fidelity in the region comes from more days. Option B still
  applies per cluster as built. Report the ±3% check for the detail region's zones.
- **Large-sample production-cost runs** (`adjust/make_split_models.py`, builds reused from the expansion run): many days
  or the full record, with `cf_fidelity` off. These must start from a build without rescaled profiles, so a split of an
  expansion case that had it on is not valid. Use the expansion case's `vcf_fidelity.csv` to attribute differences.
- **Reliability (tails):** stays with the stress-day rules and the production-cost feedback. B corrects energy, not
  extremes.
