Interconnection headroom curves for ReEDS zones

A rising cost curve for network-upgrade headroom in each of the 134 ReEDS zones, estimated from LBNL project-level interconnection costs and built into Switch. The aim is to test interconnection policy as changes to that curve.

Owner: Tom BrindleStarted: 30 Sept 2026Repo: Switch-USA-EDF, branch tom/interconnection-headroomEstimated: 1 Oct 2026 (LBNL data, PR #10)

Why this project

For new wind and solar, the binding transmission constraint over the next five years is mostly not interregional backbone. It is headroom on the existing 69–345 kV network a few hops from the point of interconnection. Under participant funding, a shortage of that headroom shows up as network-upgrade charges assigned to individual projects. These costs have risen sharply in PJM, MISO and SPP, and they are the main grid-side reason projects withdraw from queues.

Zonal capacity expansion models mostly miss this. In Switch-USA-PG-ReEDS, the network cost a new solar MW pays does not depend on how much has already been built nearby. So the model can't show a zone filling up, and it can't value reforms that make headroom cheaper or larger.

What exists already

  • ReEDS and reV trace a path along existing lines from each site's point of interconnection to the zone centre, priced at 50% of greenfield cost to stand in for reconductoring. ReEDS also applies a flat $100/kW intra-zone adder to net capacity additions, calibrated to historical gas interconnection costs. Both are fixed per site and don't rise with cumulative builds. PowerGenome passes a similar distance-based reinforcement cost into Switch.
  • LBNL has extracted project-level interconnection costs from study PDFs for MISO, PJM, SPP, NYISO, ISO-NE, CAISO and a set of non-ISO balancing areas. The costs are split into point-of-interconnection and network-upgrade components. Projects with high network-upgrade costs cluster geographically, and capacity (NRIS) service needs more network than energy-only service.
  • ReEDS also limits near-term builds with county-level queue volumes (interconnection_queues.csv). That is a quantity cap, not a cost curve.

I haven't found a published zone-level network-upgrade curve that rises with saturation and is built into a US capacity expansion model. Ask the LBNL interconnection team before going further.

Method

1. Saturation by zone and year

For each zone z and year t:

saturation(z,t) = [ Σ_tech w_tech × MW added since 2010
                    − 0.1 × Σ_tech w_tech × MW retired since 2010 ]⁺  ÷  headroom proxy(z,t)

Every new generator uses headroom, including gas. The weight w is the share of the network one MW of nameplate occupies under interconnection-study conditions, with solar as the reference at 1.0. The defaults are wind 0.25, storage 0.5, and gas and everything else 1.0, with 10% of retired capacity's headroom reusable. A retiring plant frees headroom at its own weight times that share.

The headroom proxy is the zone's boundary capacity: its BA-to-BA export capacity in NARIS 2024, the mean of the two directions summed over its interfaces. The earlier proxy summed county-to-county limits inside the zone. That counts a corridor once per county pair it crosses, so its internal-to-boundary ratio rose with county count (correlation 0.70). Load-based alternatives add the zone's coincident peak, median or 10th-percentile hourly load in each year to the boundary capacity; the generation proxy (nameplate online in 2010) is also kept.

fit-weights fits every combination of proxy (six), wind weight (0.1 to 1), gas weight (0, 0.5, 1) and reuse share (0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5), with storage fixed at 0.5, on the same sample and regimes as the main run. It ranks them by quasi-AIC: the PPML likelihood on continuous $/kW is a quasi-likelihood, so it is scaled by the overdispersion (about 450). Boundary is best. The fit's optimum wind weight is the grid's lowest, 0.1 (0.15 is 1.0 QAIC behind). The default of 0.25, 2.9 behind, is a physical floor. Boundary + p10 load is the best load-based proxy, 8 QAIC behind. Sensitivity configs (sensitivities/) cover wind 0.5, boundary + p10, and pricing at active.

Headroom use versus cost per MW

The model keeps two effects separate. The weights set how fast each technology fills a zone. The regression's technology dummies set what each technology pays per MW at a given saturation, which mostly reflects siting: gas tends to reuse brownfield sites near load and pipelines, while wind and solar sit where the resource is. Before this change, gas had a weight of zero and so never filled a zone, which tilted Switch towards gas in constrained zones.

Placing projects in zones

Each LBNL cost-data project is placed in a zone by the first of these that succeeds: the cost file's county; the Queued Up county for the same queue ID (same ISO or utility, same state); a committed crosswalk for county strings that aren't a single county; the state, if ReEDS serves it with one zone; the project's transmission owner. For the last, the owner's footprint is the zones of its other projects in the same file and state. One zone is assigned. With several, the project takes their saturation, weighted by the owner's MW in each, and is flagged. With none it is dropped. link-report prints the MW share placed by ISO and status.

Zone load data

zone_load_stats.csv holds each zone's coincident peak, median and 10th-percentile hourly load and annual MWh for 2010–2030, all on Central Standard Time. For 2016–2023 it sums NREL's hourly county load to zones. For 2010–2013 it splits ReEDS's state hourly load (Zenodo 18462671) into zones by each zone's share of its state by month and hour, from 2016–2018. That split reproduces 2019–2023 zone peaks to within 2.1% (median) and 9.5% (90th percentile); Texas zones are worst. 2014–2015 are interpolated and rescaled to EIA state sales; 2024 scales 2023 by EIA growth; 2025 onward hold 2023. Where zone sets line up with EIA-930 balancing areas (PJM, NYISO, ISO-NE, ERCOT, Nevada Power, Duke Carolinas), annual energy and peak agree within about 4% (median).

2. Cost regression

E[NU $/kW] = exp( spline(saturation) + tech + ERIS/NRIS + β·log(MW) + γ·(queue year − 2015)
                  + planning-regime FE + completed × regime )

The model is fitted by Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) on network-upgrade $/kW. That estimates the conditional mean directly, keeps zero-cost projects, and needs no retransformation; log-OLS remains an option. Each project gets its zone's saturation in the year it entered the queue. The central sample is active and completed projects (n = 2,787). Withdrawn projects overstate costs because they were studied in overloaded clusters, so they are a high-cost sensitivity. Standard errors are clustered by zone (105 clusters).

Regimes. ISOs are regimes. The non-ISO release gives utility regimes for BPA, PacifiCorp, Duke Carolinas and Duke Progress, which apply to the zones whose ReEDS hurdle region is that utility; Duke Florida is pooled into FRCC. Zones in regions with no projects (rest of NorthernGrid and SERTP, WestConnect, CAISO, ERCOT: 41 zones) take the mean regime effect.

Completed vs active. Completed projects cost much less than active ones in most regions, but the ratio varies too much for one coefficient (observed 0.07 in PacifiCorp to 1.05 in NYISO). Regimes with at least 25 completed projects get their own completed effect; the others (ISO-NE, BPA, Duke Carolinas, Duke Progress) share one. Estimated multipliers: PacifiCorp 0.24, PJM 0.42, SPP 0.57, MISO 0.87, FRCC 1.29, NYISO 3.61, pooled 0.93. Reference curves are priced at completed, with each regime's completed multiplier capped to 0.5–2 times the pooled one (tranches.status_pricing: capped): NYISO 3.61 → 1.72, PJM 0.42 → 0.46, PacifiCorp 0.24 → 0.46; MISO, SPP and FRCC are inside the band. The regime effects span ×15 and NYISO's rests on 28 completed projects, so the cap keeps each effect's direction while bounding it. Pricing every zone at the pooled effect is the sensitivity (status_pooled.yaml); it moves PJM's first step up ×2 and NYISO's down ×2.

Trend. Costs rise 0.115 log points per queue year (×1.12 a year). Curves evaluate the trend at each regime's last queue year in the sample, not at 2024 (tranches.trend_freeze: per_regime): MISO, PJM, SPP and NYISO at 2020, ISO-NE 2021, the non-ISO regimes 2023, and zones with no regime in the sample at 2023. The ISO workbooks end in 2020 (ISO-NE 2021), only 8 completed projects were queued after 2021 (all Duke Energy Florida), and evaluating at 2024 moved completed-project costs ×2.9 from the sample's mean queue year (2013).

Saturation. Knots at 0.25 and 0.5 are kept (13.5 QAIC better than a straight line). The slope is 3.03 (s.e. 0.98) up to 0.25 and flattens beyond it; the knot terms are not individually significant. Other effects: ERIS −0.38, wind +0.36, trend +0.11 a year. Deviance pseudo-R² 0.34. Across region × technology cells of completed projects with at least 10 projects, predicted means are 0.50–1.79 times observed.

3. Tranches

Each zone's curve starts at its 2026 saturation: the last EIA year plus planned additions and announced retirements. It runs only to the edge of the data, the 98th percentile of sample saturation (0.67), in equal steps of at most 0.015. Each step is priced for a reference project (150 MW solar, NRIS, completed, queue year frozen at the regime's last sample year) under the zone's regime effect and its regime's capped completed effect. Prices are forced to be non-decreasing, so the curve is convex and the LP fills it in order. The 20 zones already past the edge get one step 0.05 wide at the edge price. Beyond the curve, headroom comes only from building network capacity: new intra-zonal lines are available in every scenario from 2030 as the backstop. Their cost is full new build: the zone's capacity-weighted median ReEDS reinforcement cost (interconnection_land.h5 at the pinned ReEDS commit, 2023$ → 2024$, weighted by UPV and onshore-wind site capacity) divided by 0.5. ReEDS prices reinforcement routes "with capital expenditure (CAPEX) costs multiplied by 50% to approximate the lower cost for reconductoring compared to greenfield transmission construction" (ReEDS docs/source/model_documentation.md, "Network reinforcement", line 1809), so its cost is a reconductoring cost: reconductoring is priced at it as is, and a new line at twice it. The cap is 10 × base capacity, a finite bound for the LP that should not bind.

New-line units. ReEDS traces a path along existing lines from each wind or solar POI to the zone centre, prices it on its transmission cost surface (CAPEX × 0.5 for reconductoring), and charges that cost for every MW(AC) of new capacity at the POI; its own accounting counts reinforcement as new GW × route distance. So ReEDS's $/MW is per MW of generation delivered to the zone centre. Because that cost is set at 50% of new-build CAPEX, a new line is priced at twice it. In host mode (default) the new line is priced per weighted MW of headroom it hosts, with no conversion; across zones the median is $639/kW (10th / 90th percentile $355 / $1,299; it was $319 before the reconductoring discount was removed). In stretch mode the line adds network capacity H, so its cost is converted to $ per MW of H by reinforcement.per_mw_h (default curve_end: × curve-end saturation; alternatives s0 and gen are sensitivities).

Reference curves cover 609 GW of headroom at base capacity (600 GW within the data plus 8.5 GW of edge steps). The median first step is $129/kW (quartiles $93–189) and the median last step $167/kW. Before the trend freeze and status cap (Oct 2026, CHANGES §41) they were $177/kW ($133–212) and $227/kW; sensitivities/previous_defaults.yaml reproduces them.

Switch formulation

The module is switch/study_modules/interconnection_headroom.py in Switch-USA-EDF; pg_to_switch.py writes its inputs when pg/settings/interconnection_headroom.yml is enabled.

Each ReEDS BA is an interconnection zone with explicit network capacity H, starting from the headroom proxy H0 (boundary capacity by default). Uprate caps and "% network added" are shares of H0, so they follow the chosen proxy. The cost curve is stored in units of fullness: step k is wk wide and costs ck per MW of generation. For each zone and period:

H                = H0 + MW of stretch uprates built (GETs, reconductoring)
step k           ≤ w_k × H                          (stretch uprates stretch every step)
released         ≤ s0 × MW of stretch uprates built (existing use fills less of a bigger network)
hosted           = MW of new lines built (host mode, the default) + hosted MW carried from earlier stages

Σ w_g · new capacity of g   ≤   initial headroom
                              + reuse × Σ w_g × existing capacity retired or suspended
                              + Σ steps + released + hosted
  • All of this is linear, so the model stays an LP. The released term is exact for small uprates and slightly conservative for large ones; an exact version would need integer variables.
  • Each uprate option has a cap (share of H0), a cost per MW and a first available year. GETs and reconductoring raise the capacity of the existing network, so they stretch the curve (cost per MW of H). New lines, by default (new_line_mode: host), host generation directly: each MW adds one weighted MW of headroom at the zone's ReEDS reinforcement cost per MW of generation, with no empirical step or release cost on top, and leave H, the steps and the release unchanged; they are capped at 10 × H0. Steps, released headroom and uprates are annualised over 40 years at the model interest rate.
  • Why host mode. The new-line cost is the full cost of delivering a MW of new generation to the zone centre. When a new line stretched the curve, generation on the stretched steps also paid those steps' LBNL network-upgrade costs and the release cost, counting network upgrades twice. On the toy, a zone past the data edge (s0 0.8, one 0.05 edge step at $50/kW) with a $200/kW line prices marginal headroom at $200/kW in host mode against $250/kW in stretch mode (the line's $170 per MW of H at curve-end saturation 0.85, plus 0.8 × $50 release and 0.05 × $50 step, per 0.85 MW); this stacking is a likely part of the ~×2 scarcity rent the VM found past the edge. Stretch mode stays available (new_line_mode: stretch, sensitivities/new_line_stretch.yaml).
  • In myopic runs, new-line headroom left unused at the end of a stage carries into the next as free hosted headroom (ic_hosted_headroom_mw); hosted generation does not raise the next stage's start saturation, since it sits on the new lines, not on H.
  • Every new build uses headroom at its technology weight; distributed generation never does. Only NEW_GEN_BLD_YRS count, because existing capacity is already reflected in where each curve starts.
  • Under state or interconnect aggregation, each aggregate load zone keeps its BAs as separate interconnection zones and pools their headroom.

What it reports

  • ic_spend.csv: overnight and annual reinforcement spend by zone, period and type (reactive upgrades along the curve, GETs, reconductoring, new lines, each its own row), with the network MW added and generation MW enabled (for new lines: the hosted MW).
  • ic_network.csv: base network capacity and the MW and % added by GETs and reconductoring (deliberate_mw_added); headroom hosted by new lines (hosted_mw) and the network capacity those lines imply, new_line_network_mw_implied = hosted MW / the zone's curve-end saturation (curve_end_saturation = s0 + the step widths: weighted generation per MW of network at the end of the empirical curve). Uprate MW are model decisions. Reactive MW are an estimate: the curve records what generators paid, so the report divides that spend by an engineering cost of network capacity (a placeholder) and shows the implied network MW per generation MW as a sanity check.
  • ic_headroom.csv: headroom used, freed and bought per load zone, with the constraint dual.

On the Switch three-zone toy, North needed more room for wind than its cheap first step allowed. Instead of buying the $3M/MW second step, the model built 1 MW of GETs and 2.5 MW of reconductoring. That raised North's network capacity 35% and stretched the cheap step from 2.0 to 2.7 MW, and existing use filling less of the network released another 1.05 MW. Total system cost fell 1.2% against the run without uprate options.

Before running it in Switch-USA-PG, confirm that PowerGenome's reinforcement cost (tx_capex) is removed from wind and solar connection costs, keeping spur lines. pg_to_switch.py does this when the setting is on, and writes ic_connect_cost_check.csv to verify it.

Open issues

  • Beyond the data. Curves stop at the 98th percentile of sample saturation (0.67). Twenty zones, mostly in ERCOT, CAISO, FRCC and WestConnect, already sit past it and rely on one edge step plus the new-line backstop, now priced at full new-build cost (ReEDS's reinforcement cost for the zone / 0.5); its start year (2030) is still a placeholder. Those zones' results depend on that cost.
  • Small regimes. BPA, Duke Carolinas, Duke Progress and ISO-NE have 2–8 completed projects, so they share one completed effect. Their completed predictions are 0.5–3 times observed on very few projects.
  • Wind weight. The fit keeps preferring the lowest wind weight tried. 0.25 is set as a physical floor; the wind = 0.5 sensitivity shows how much this matters (592 GW within support vs 600 GW).
  • Endogenous proxy. NARIS 2024 limits include lines built because of IBR, such as CREZ. That lowers measured saturation exactly where development has been heaviest. A pre-2010 network snapshot would be cleaner if one exists.
  • Retirement reuse and load growth. Reuse is now 0.1 (see above). Load growth enters only through the load-based proxies, which are sensitivities.
  • Regime coverage. 41 zones (rest of NorthernGrid and SERTP, WestConnect, CAISO, ERCOT) use the mean regime effect and the pooled completed effect. ERCOT uses connect-and-manage, so its cost appears as congestion rather than upgrade charges; the hub version below handles that properly.
  • Project location. MISO, PJM and SPP cost files have no county. Projects are placed by queue ID in LBNL Queued Up; the rest by a short crosswalk, single-zone states, or their transmission owner's footprint. 28 rows are placed only by an owner spanning several zones; they take a capacity-weighted mean saturation and are flagged (ba_multi). 28 rows (mostly SPP rows with no transmission owner) can't be placed and are dropped. ISO-NE offshore wind projects (no county) take their landing state's single zone. Where the cost file and Queued Up both give a county they name the same county for 97% of projects and the same zone for 99.5%.
  • Withdrawal selection. The completed sample is survivor-biased and the withdrawn sample is cluster-inflated. Report results under both.
